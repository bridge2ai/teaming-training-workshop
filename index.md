# Team Science Training Workshop

## Workshop Overview

Welcome to the Team Science Training Workshop! Developed by the Bridge2AI Teaming and Collaboration Working Group, this half-day (about four hours), modular workshop helps interdisciplinary research teams move from understanding team science concepts to applying them. It focuses on building *social infrastructure*: the tangible teaming artifacts (such as communication charters and data sharing agreements) and everyday routines that make multi-institutional collaboration work.

The curriculum draws on Kolb's experiential learning theory and the principles of andragogy (adult learning). Each module pairs a short, evidence-based content block with a hands-on activity that produces a concrete output. The workshop ends with the "Monday Morning Protocol," a personal plan of low-stakes micro-pilots designed to close the "knowing-doing gap."

## Workshop Goals

This training workshop aims to equip participants with the knowledge, skills, and tools needed to:

### 1. Understand Team Science Fundamentals
- Learn what makes research teams effective based on scientific evidence
- Identify common failure modes in collaborative research
- Apply the IMPACT framework (Interdependence, Motivation, Processes, Abilities, Culture, Tools) to team dynamics

### 2. Build Communication Architecture
- Learn the basics of effective communication
- Learn how to design and implement successful communication architectures for team science
- Apply what you’ve learned to create your own Communication Charter

### 3. Design Governance Structures
- Understand different governance and leadership models (traditional hierarchy, distributed leadership, network governance)
- Map decision-making layers in a large consortium and identify where you have agency within them
- "Fishbowl" fireside chat with leaders and experts from around the country

### 4. Manage Data and Resources Collaboratively
- Apply FAIR+ principles (Findable, Accessible, Interoperable, Reusable, plus security, ethics, and sustainability) to team data practices
- Negotiate data sharing agreements that balance protection with access
- Establish protocols for resource allocation and management

### 5. Build Effective Interdisciplinary Teams
- Distinguish surface-level from deep-level heterogeneity in team composition
- Place your team on the monodisciplinary-to-transdisciplinary continuum and use Thompson's framework to match coordination to interdependence
- Assess your team against Hackman's 5 conditions and Edmondson's principles of psychological safety

### 6. Implement Sustainable Practices
- Address the Knowing-Doing Gap: training knowledge is often lost without implementation. 
- Draft a "Monday Morning Protocol": design a Micro-Pilot to address a single friction point in a team. Zero budget, no permission required.
- Learn to ensure measurement and scaling. 


## Training Format

The workshop consists of six interactive modules combining:

- **Evidence-based content:** Research findings on team effectiveness
- **Hands-on activities:** Practical exercises and simulations
- **Group discussions:** Peer learning and knowledge sharing
- **Action planning:** Concrete steps for implementation

## Target Audience

This training is designed for:

- Research team members at all career stages: trainees, early-stage investigators, consortium leaders, and principal investigators
- Informatics project managers and data coordinators who handle the social complexities of data sharing and resource management
- Anyone involved in multi-institutional or interdisciplinary research

Participants do not need to be on the same research team. The first edition focused on trainees (undergraduate and postbaccalaureate trainees, graduate students, postdoctoral fellows, and research staff).

## Expected Outcomes

By the end of this workshop, participants will:

- Have a clear understanding of what makes research teams succeed or struggle
- Possess practical frameworks and tools for improving team collaboration
- Have developed personal action plans for implementing specific practices
- Be connected with peers for ongoing learning and support

## Workshop History

The first edition was delivered in person on February 13, 2026, at the University of Colorado Anschutz Medical Campus by six facilitators from the Bridge2AI Teaming and Collaboration Working Group. Later versions were held in Bethesda for government program officials and for attendees of the [Bridge2AI Fall 2026 All Hands Meeting and Open House](https://meeting.bridge2ai.org/). The content continues to be adapted based on participant feedback.
